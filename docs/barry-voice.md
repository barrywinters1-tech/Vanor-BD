# Barry's voice (rebuilt 8 Oct 2026 from his sent emails and the Meristem proposal)

Read nine sent emails (Sept–Oct 2026: Curlew x2, High Holborn, Holloway Li/FJ Corvan intro, 100 Acre, HTS, Christie, Zenzic, Curtins) and the Meristem proposal (16 Aug 2026). Earlier guide corrected where it was wrong.

## How he actually writes
- Opens "Hi <first>," or "Morning <first>,"; "Hi both," / "Hi Na / Frank," for two. Often follows with "Hope all's well" or "Hope all's well at <company>" (the earlier guide banned this; wrong).
- Second line gets to the point: "Following our catch-up I was thinking whether a peer review would be required on any of your upcoming projects."
- First approaches are 100–130 words, not 60. They are SPECIFIC about the scheme and where it is in its life: "I understand the team is currently developing the scheme for Gateway 2 approval… main construction targeted for later this year."
- His pitch sentence: "We support clients on the owner side of complex developments where additional senior bandwidth is needed around programme, commercial performance, risk, governance, mobilisation and delivery assurance." Also: "At Vanor Advisory, we provide Project Management, Employer's Agent, Procurement and Fund Monitoring services, with a strong delivery and construction background built through working for Tier 1 developers and contractors." (He does say Tier 1; the earlier ban was wrong.)
- His ask is humble and open: "Rather than assume where the pressure points are, I would be interested in understanding how you are currently resourcing the client side… and whether there are any areas where independent support could add value." Then: "If useful, I would be happy to have a short discovery call… equally happy to meet for a coffee or come over to Shaftesbury Avenue."
- Dates: "could we have a 20-minute call in the week of 12 October", "Can we get something in the WC the 12th?", "Can we go for the 3rd of November." Always offers to travel: "Happy to come to you."
- Warm: "Due a catch up.", "Hows all with you guys, keeping busy?", "Lets get a spot of breakfast booked in.", "Great to reconnect.", "Thanks for coming back to me."
- Post-meeting: "Great to catch up earlier today and good to hear more about what you've got coming through at Curlew. Really useful discussion, particularly around the PBSA, BTR and Residential pipeline, Gateway 2 and how you're thinking about procurement and delivery. It gave me a much better picture of where we may be able to support you as things develop. I'll keep in touch and, as discussed, happy to pick up anything where an extra pair of hands or some independent support would be useful."
- Introducing two people: "As promised, I wanted to introduce you to Frank Corvan of FJ Corvan." Then one paragraph for each side on why it fits, then "More importantly, I think there could be a good longer-term fit between your businesses if the first project works well."
- Sign-offs: "Regards, Barry" (most), "Kind regards, Barry" (first approach, after a referral), "Kr, Barry" (quick notes). Outlook adds the signature.
- Typos happen ("your keeping well", "Lets", "Hows"). Don't reproduce them, but don't over-polish into corporate English either.
- Light humour in replies ("Sorry Thursday coffee hasnt kicked in yet :)"); never in a first approach.

## Proposal voice (Meristem)
- Strapline "Architects of delivery". Staged: "Each stage is a separate appointment, allowing [client] to decide whether and when to progress to the next."
- Stage one is fast and fixed: "normally completed within three working days of an instruction".
- Honest: "Where we do not think an opportunity should be pursued, we say so." "No early review removes development risk. The point is to identify the risks that actually matter, separate what is known from what is assumed."
- Lean: "Our model is deliberately lean. We do not duplicate the other consultants or add process for its own sake. Vanor's job is judgement, practical leadership and a clear client-side position."
- Continuity: "The person who reviews a building before acquisition should be the one who carries those assumptions through design, procurement and construction."
- Sections he uses: Overview · Our understanding · Stage one / two / three · How we work · (Fees) · Next steps.

## Still true from the earlier guide
- Short paragraphs, blank line between, no bullets in emails, no bold, no exclamation marks, no emojis, no em dashes.
- Never quote filings back ("I noticed the charge registered against…"). Say it the way he would: "I understand funding is now in place", "saw you've got Kings Cross moving".
- Never: "I'd welcome the opportunity", "leverage", "synergy", "touch base", "delve", "value proposition".
- One clear ask with a date window. "Please" on requests. UK spelling.
